A 14-Week Open Course for Clinical Social Workers

Curated by Topher Rasmussen, MSW

Most MSW programs treat psychodynamic theory as a historical footnote. A week on Freud, maybe two. A passing reference to object relations before moving on to CBT worksheets and manualized protocols. If you’re lucky, someone mentions transference. If you’re very lucky, they explain it.

This is a problem. Not because every social worker needs to become a psychoanalyst, but because psychodynamic thinking is the deepest framework we have for understanding what actually happens between two people in a room. It’s the tradition that takes the unconscious seriously. That treats the therapeutic relationship not as a delivery mechanism for interventions, but as the intervention itself. That asks you to sit with ambiguity, tolerate not-knowing, and trust that something meaningful is happening even when you can’t name it yet.

I built this course because I wanted that training and couldn’t find it. During my MSW at the University of Utah, I designed a 14-week independent study with Dr. Joanna Bettmann Schaefer — a deep, sustained immersion in psychodynamic thought from Freud through contemporary relational and intersubjective theories. Not a survey. A transformation.

Now I’m making it available to anyone who wants it. The entire course — the syllabus, the curator framing, the audio overviews, the reflection prompts — is open and free. You can work through it at your own pace, on your own terms, without paying anything or talking to anyone. If you want the full readings and a live cohort experience, that option exists too. But the core of what I built is here, right now, for you.

This is for pre-licensed social workers who suspect their programs left something out. For clinicians who want to understand why their client keeps recreating the same relational pattern in the therapy room. For anyone who’s felt the limits of “What are your coping skills?” and wants a deeper way to think about what’s happening.

What You’ll Leave With

Not a certificate. Not a credential. Something more useful: a way of thinking you didn’t have before.

After fourteen weeks with this material, you’ll carry a psychodynamic lens — an orientation toward clinical work that notices transference, holds countertransference, and understands defense not as pathology but as adaptation. You’ll have a clinical vocabulary drawn from Freud, Klein, Winnicott, Kohut, Ogden, Bromberg, and the relational tradition. You’ll think differently about what happens in the space between you and your clients.

The course traces the evolution of psychodynamic thought across a century, always with an eye toward what this means for the clinician sitting across from another person. Special attention is given to the intersection of race, class, gender, culture, and biology with inner psychological life — the biopsychosocial person-in-environment perspective that defines our profession.

How It Works

Self-paced. The course was designed for fourteen weeks, but it’s yours to move through however you want. Some people will take a week per module. Others will read one paper a day and finish in a month. There’s no deadline, no one checking your progress.

Each week includes: a curator framing paragraph that tells you why this material matters clinically, full reading citations with links to request copies, media recommendations, and two to three reflection prompts designed for solo journaling or group discussion.

Open source. Everything here is visible and free. The readings are cited but not hosted — most are behind journal paywalls or in books still in print. If you want a specific reading, click “request” and I’ll help you find it. That’s also how you’ll hear about the live consultation groups I’m putting together.

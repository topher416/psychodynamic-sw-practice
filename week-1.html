Week 01

What Is Psychodynamic Psychotherapy, and Is It Evidence-Based?

Before we get into the theories themselves, we need to answer the question that every social worker gets asked: does this stuff actually work? Shedler’s 2010 paper is the single best argument for psychodynamic therapy’s empirical standing, and the Berzoff chapter grounds us in why a biopsychosocial lens matters in the first place.

Required Readings

Berzoff, J., Flanagan, L. M. & Hertz, P. — Ch. 1: Why a Biopsychosocial Context? Inside Out and Outside In. (13 pgs) request

Shedler, J. (2010). The efficacy of psychodynamic psychotherapy. American Psychologist, 65, 98–109. (11 pgs) request

Reflection Prompts

  1. If a colleague or supervisor told you that psychodynamic therapy isn’t evidence-based, how would you respond? What does Shedler’s argument change about how you think about “evidence” in clinical work?
  2. Think about a client you’re currently working with. How does a biopsychosocial lens — one that holds biology, psychology, and social context together — change the way you understand what’s happening for them?
  3. What drew you to psychodynamic ideas in the first place? What are you hoping to gain from this course that your MSW program didn’t provide?

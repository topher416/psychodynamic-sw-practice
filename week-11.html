Week 11

Clinical Application — Rupture and Repair

Every therapeutic relationship breaks. The question is whether you can repair it — and whether the repair itself becomes the treatment. Muran and Eubanks have done the most rigorous empirical work on alliance ruptures, and their writing is refreshingly jargon-free.

Required Readings

Muran, J. C., Eubanks, C. F., & Samstag, L. W. (2021). One more time with less jargon. J. Clinical Psychology, 77, 361–368. (7 pgs) request

Eubanks, C. F., Muran, J. C., & Safran, J. D. (2018). Alliance rupture repair: A meta-analysis. Psychotherapy, 55(4), 508–519. (11 pgs) request

Supplemental Readings

Friedlander, M. L. (2015). Use of relational strategies to repair alliance ruptures. Psychotherapy, 52(2), 174–179. (6 pgs) request

A Note on Readings

Reflection Prompts

  1. Think about a moment when you felt a client pull away — canceling sessions, going silent, becoming hostile, or becoming overly compliant. Looking back, can you identify the rupture that preceded it? What was happening in the relationship that you might not have named at the time?
  2. Muran and Eubanks distinguish between withdrawal ruptures and confrontation ruptures. Which type do you tend to notice more easily in your clients? Which type do you tend to enact yourself as a clinician?
  3. How do you currently handle moments when a client tells you — directly or indirectly — that you got it wrong? What would it look like to treat that moment not as a problem to fix, but as the most important clinical material in the room?

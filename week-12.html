Week 12

The Relational Turn

The relational turn changed everything. It moved psychodynamic therapy away from the analyst-as-blank-screen toward the idea that the therapeutic relationship is a mutual, co-constructed process. Ornstein and Ganzer’s paper makes the case for why this matters specifically for social work.

Ornstein, E. D. & Ganzer, C. (2005). Relational social work: A model for the future. Families in Society, 86, 565–572. (7 pgs) request

Reflection Prompts

  1. The relational turn argues that the therapist is never a blank screen — that who you are always shapes the clinical encounter. Think about a recent session: what parts of yourself were present in the room, whether you intended them to be or not? How did your subjectivity influence what happened?
  2. Ornstein and Ganzer argue that relational theory is a natural fit for social work. Where do you see the overlap between relational psychodynamic thinking and the person-in-environment perspective you were trained in? Where do they diverge?
  3. Consider a moment in your clinical work when you chose to be more “real” with a client — disclosing something about your experience, departing from a neutral stance, or simply letting yourself be affected. What happened next? How do you understand that moment now?

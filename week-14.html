Week 14

From Theory to Practice

We end where the work begins — in the room, with real people, in messy contexts. Ngo-Smith’s paper on psychoanalysis and homelessness is a necessary corrective to the fantasy of the idealized consulting room. Ogden’s “Ontological Psychoanalysis” asks what kind of clinician you want to become.

A Note on Readings

The readings listed here are cited but not hosted. Many are behind journal paywalls or published in books still in print. Click “request” next to any reading to send me a pre-filled email, and I’ll do what I can to help you find it.

Reflection Prompts

  1. Ngo-Smith writes about doing psychoanalytic work in settings where the frame is constantly under siege — shelters, streets, chaotic environments. How does her paper challenge or expand your assumptions about where and how psychodynamic work can happen? What does it mean for your own practice setting?
  2. Ogden asks what kind of clinician you want to become — not what techniques you want to master, but who you want to be in the room. After fourteen weeks with this material, how would you answer that question? What has shifted in how you think about your clinical identity?
  3. Looking back across the full arc of this course — from Freud to intersubjectivity, from the evidence base to the frame, from Klein’s positions to the relational turn — which idea has most changed the way you sit with clients? Which one are you still wrestling with?

If you’ve made it here — whether over fourteen weeks or fourteen days — you’ve done something most MSW programs never ask of their students. You’ve sat with dense, sometimes disorienting ideas and let them work on you. The psychodynamic lens isn’t something you learn once. It’s something you carry into every session, every supervision, every moment of clinical uncertainty. That’s the transformation this course was built for.

If you want to keep going — working through this material with other clinicians in a live consultation group — I’d love to hear from you.

Week 02

Origins of Psychotherapy — Freud

Love him or not, you have to understand Freud to understand everything that came after. This week covers drive theory, the unconscious, and the basic architecture of psychoanalytic thought. Freud’s 1914 paper on remembering, repeating, and working through remains one of the most clinically useful things he ever wrote.

Reflection Prompts

  1. Think about a client who keeps returning to the same relational patterns — the same kind of partner, the same conflict at work, the same rupture with a friend. How does Freud’s concept of the repetition compulsion help you understand what might be happening beneath the surface?
  2. Freud is often dismissed in social work education as outdated or reductive. After reading the material this week, what do you think is worth keeping from Freud, and what do you think deserves the critique? How does that change what you bring into the room?
  3. McWilliams asks what defines a psychoanalytic therapy. When you think about your own clinical work, what elements of a psychodynamic approach are you already practicing — even if you haven’t been calling them that?

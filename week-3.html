Week 03

Clinical Application — Transference and Countertransference

This is where theory meets the room. Transference and countertransference are the engine of psychodynamic work, and this week brings together four essential perspectives — Joseph on the total situation, Loewald on the mutuality of the process, Ogden on the first meeting, and Winnicott’s deceptively brief paper that reframes the whole concept.

Reflection Prompts

  1. Think about a client who evokes a strong emotional reaction in you — frustration, protectiveness, boredom, dread before the session. What might your countertransference be telling you about what the client is communicating nonverbally or reenacting in the therapeutic relationship?
  2. Joseph argues that transference is not just what the patient says about you, but the “total situation” — everything happening in the room. How does this expanded view of transference change the way you listen in session? What would you start paying attention to that you might currently overlook?
  3. Ogden focuses on the very first meeting. Think about your most recent intake or initial session. What was happening between you and the client before either of you said a word? What transferential material was already present in how they entered the room, sat down, or began speaking?

Week 05

Race and Racism in Psychodynamic Theory

Psychodynamic theory has a complicated relationship with race. This week confronts that head-on — examining how racism operates in the consulting room, in countertransference, and within the psychoanalytic tradition itself. If psychodynamic social work is going to remain relevant, it has to reckon with who it was built by and who it has historically excluded.

Reflection Prompts

  1. Shah writes about racism as an “unbearable mental state” in the countertransference. Think about a moment in your clinical work when race was present in the room but went unspoken. What made it hard to name? What do you think was lost by not addressing it?
  2. Consider your own social location — your race, ethnicity, class background, and the communities you come from. How might these shape the way you hear a client’s material, what you notice, and what you unconsciously avoid? Where might your blind spots live?
  3. The Holmes Commission report describes the experience of race “on the couch.” If psychoanalysis was historically built by and for white European clinicians, what does it mean to practice psychodynamically with clients whose experiences were never centered in the theory? How do you hold that tension in your work?

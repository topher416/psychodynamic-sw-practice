Week 06

Object Relations — Melanie Klein

Klein is dense, strange, and brilliant. Her ideas about the paranoid-schizoid and depressive positions, about envy and gratitude, about the way we split the world into good and bad before we can hold it as whole — these are some of the most clinically generative concepts in all of psychodynamic theory. Give her time.

Reflection Prompts

  1. Klein describes the move from the paranoid-schizoid position to the depressive position as a developmental achievement — the capacity to hold ambivalence, to see the other as whole rather than split into all-good or all-bad. Think about a client who seems stuck in splitting. What would it look like for them to begin moving toward the depressive position, and what might your role be in that process?
  2. Klein’s concept of envy is often misunderstood as simple jealousy. She means something deeper — the impulse to spoil or destroy the very thing that sustains you. Have you encountered this dynamic in your clinical work? What did it feel like in the room, and how did you respond?
  3. How do you see Klein’s ideas about mourning showing up in the losses your clients carry — not just bereavement, but losses of identity, of safety, of the way they thought their life would go? How might Kleinian mourning differ from the way grief is typically addressed in social work?

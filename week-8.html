Week 08

Object Relations — Donald Woods Winnicott

Winnicott is the theorist who makes social workers feel seen. The good-enough mother, the holding environment, the capacity to be alone in the presence of another — his ideas are deeply humane and immediately applicable to clinical work. Ogden’s essay on reading Winnicott is itself a masterclass in how to think psychodynamically.

Reflection Prompts

  1. Winnicott’s “Hate in the Countertransference” gives clinicians permission to acknowledge something rarely spoken about openly — that there are moments when we genuinely hate our clients. Think about a client who has evoked something close to hatred in you. What was happening in the treatment at that point, and how did you manage it? What would Winnicott say about the clinical importance of being able to know and hold that hate?
  2. The concept of the “good-enough mother” suggests that perfection is not only unnecessary but harmful — that what matters is reliable, imperfect attunement. How does this idea apply to your clinical work? Where do you put pressure on yourself to be a “perfect” therapist, and what might it mean to be good enough instead?
  3. Kanter’s paper extends Winnicottian ideas beyond the consulting room into community care. Think about the settings where you practice or have practiced — agencies, hospitals, shelters, schools. How does the concept of a “holding environment” translate when the frame is porous, the setting is chaotic, and the client may not have asked to be there?

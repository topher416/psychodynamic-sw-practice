Week 09

Clinical Application — The Frame

The frame is what makes therapy therapy. This week examines what the frame is, why it matters, and what happens when it bends or breaks — which, in social work settings, it does constantly. McWilliams and Busch provide the practical grounding, while Bleger and Bass push into the deeper meaning of the frame itself.

Reflection Prompts

  1. Think about a time the frame shifted or broke in your clinical work — a missed session, a boundary crossed, a policy that forced a disruption. What did that moment reveal about the therapeutic relationship that might have stayed hidden otherwise?
  2. In social work settings, the “ideal” analytic frame is often impossible — sessions happen in shelters, hospitals, homes, and hallways. How do you hold a psychodynamic frame when the setting itself resists one? What is the minimum frame that still allows the work to happen?
  3. Consider a client on your current caseload who tests boundaries. How might you understand their behavior not as resistance, but as communication about what the frame means to them — and what its absence has meant in their life?
